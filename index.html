Trend board

AI chip factories are still dramatically under-built for the demand. That scarcity is why compute is getting dearer, why old cards still earn, and why the money is moving to whoever owns plants, power, and chips. The smartest two labs can still charge for the model. Everyone else is selling tokens, rent, or software a chatbot can replace. China is catching up through data and open weights, and by locking the ugly supply chains the West forgot. July’s stock scare did not show up in the factories.

14 Aug 2026. Heat is what the world is doing, plus who is talking. Factory shortage and dearer compute sit in Rising with the rest. Nothing peaking. Nothing killed.

Rising

15
AI chip factories are still dramatically under-built
The plants that make the chips were not built fast enough for the demand now hitting them. That shortage, not a China-invasion story, is still the brake. It is why wafers are scarce, why landlords can charge, and why the build is reaching for weirder money. New tapes this week did not kill it. They keep running into the same wall.
Force: they did not build enough plants. Supply and demand, not political theater.
From: the TSMC story you already kept. Fed this week by All-In, Dylan Patel, Stratechery inference shift. Sits under cards 1, 6, and 11.
16
Compute is getting dearer, and old cards still earn
Useful intelligence is getting more expensive, not cheaper. An H100 is worth more today than three years ago. When a model got ten times cheaper, people used it thirteen times more. A six-year write-off looks wrong because the cards stay in the money long after the accountant is done with them. This is the same scarcity as the under-built factories, seen from the price of a chip instead of the missing plant.
Force: more demand for the same scarce machines, and the machines last.
From: the compute-dearer story you already kept, plus your note that cards stay useful. Enforced this week by Atallah, Dylan Patel, Stratechery. Sits under cards 3 and 9.
1
Computer landlords take the money
Google is putting cash into data-center plants, not into the next model. The model scientists are leaving. Cloud wants to rent those same chips to Anthropic. SpaceX is standing up a chip-rental business next to Starlink. The people who own the building and the power are becoming the real AI company.
Force: capital follows the scarce physical thing (plants, power, chips), not the research team.
From: All-In 8 Aug (Friedberg, Gerstner). Stratechery: Google the capital company, Microsoft and neo-clouds. BG2: SpaceX as must-own.
2
Two-tier intelligence
Anthropic and OpenAI can still charge for the model itself. Labs a year behind cannot. They only charge for computers and consulting. Cheap and Chinese open models take most of the tokens. Money and tokens have split into two markets.
Force: the frontier is still scarce. Everything below it is a commodity.
From: All-In (Sacks). 20VC Atallah. BG2 (frontier keeps ~90% of the dollars). Stratechery on Chinese models.
3
Cheap tokens feed the plants
When a model got ten times cheaper, people used it thirteen times more. July’s scare about Chinese open models is good for chip and power landlords, not a kill. The tokens still need the same machines. Margin moves from the lab to the plant.
Force: cheaper intelligence creates more work, not a smaller pie.
From: 20VC Atallah (Luna). Gavin Baker 4 Aug. All-In Friedberg on mixing cheap and expensive models.

Emerging

4
No-code software is the kill zone
Airtable sold for about a tenth of its 2021 peak, still profitable. Sacks says do not call this the death of all business software. The slice getting eaten is the tool you learned instead of hiring a programmer. A chatbot now replaces that learning curve. Software glued into government and compliance is a different animal. A software company that only hits its old plan is already destroying value, because the AI-native ones grow past it.
Force: if the product was “easier than code,” the model is now the product.
From: All-In Airtable. Vishria 11 Aug (hitting plan destroys equity).
5
China catches up through data, not invasion
American shops are selling labeled, expert-corrected training data to Chinese labs. Jason says that last-mile correction is the real catch-up, same family as copying a model. Sacks says it is a commodity, not like the 2019 chip-machine ban. Gerstner: Washington only keeps that view while the US is winning. Atallah says US companies fear OpenAI’s data policy more than a Chinese open model they can run themselves.
Force: the race is about packaged human judgment and open weights, not a blockade story.
From: All-In close. 20VC Atallah. Stratechery: Who’s afraid of Chinese models.
6
The AI build is using weirder money
Labs pay 3 to 5 times for scarce chips. Sellers finance buyers. Nvidia is reaching into insurance floats and pension money, not just cash and ordinary borrowing. Gavin says breaking a long memory contract can zero your future supply. This is still mostly talk plus a few structures. The factories are still being paid. The fragility is if revenue does not show up after the exotic paper does.
Force: when cash is not enough, the stack starts lending to itself.
From: All-In (Gurley circular). Stratechery 11 Aug Nvidia’s risky business. Gavin Baker on credit wrappers.
8
Agents can do a real back-office job
A standard “can it use a computer” test went from 42% to 85%, past the human line, after February 2026. The work that holds up is boring and repeatable: hitting portals, closing tickets, following a protocol. Buyers already think the model is good enough. They pay for the harness. Clicking is cheap. The moat is one company’s private mess: who to call, how to check, which login. A smarter model does not fix a process whose answer arrives as a phone call two days later.
Force: the bottleneck moved from “can it click” to “does this company know its own process.”
From: a16z 10 Aug, Can agents use a computer yet.
9
Older chips start to matter again
Training wanted the newest chip. Running models for users, especially agents with no human in the loop, can use a mixed bag, including older factory nodes. An H100 is worth more today than three years ago. This is the same story you already kept: cards stay useful longer than a six-year write-off. It also feeds the landlords: more of the installed base stays in the money.
Force: once the job is answering, not training, yesterday’s machine is still a machine.
From: Stratechery inference shift. Dylan Patel (H100). Enforces your compute-dearer note, does not reopen it.
10
China is locking the ugly supply chains
China stopped exporting research monkeys. Prices jumped, US labs ran short. It already refines most rare earths and is moving to own the robots that run the mines, not just the rocks. In the Iran oil shock it quietly cut imports from stockpiles the West cannot see. The FCC just swept robot vacuums and humanoids onto a national-security import list. This is not the TSMC-plants story. It is everything around the plants that nobody built a second source for.
Force: whoever owns the unglamorous input (animals, mine software, oil in tanks, household robots) can turn it off.
From: ChinaTalk 14 Jul–13 Aug (monkeys, minerals, oil, FCC robots).
11
Three brakes, no winner yet
Vishria says energy is the intelligence bottleneck, and China adds far more power than the US next year. Dylan Patel says power can scale, and the 2030 bind is the rarest chip-making machines. Elon and Stratechery say put the plants in space because permits on the ground are the real wall. Dylan says space chips are not this decade. These cannot all be the only brake. The board keeps them as a fight.
Force: three different scarce things are being sold as “the” limit. The world will pick by what actually fails first.
From: Vishria 11 Aug. Dylan Patel 13 Mar. Dwarkesh Elon 5 Feb. Stratechery SpaceX IPO.
12
Labs versus the state
The US moved to stop working with Anthropic and later ordered it to cut its strongest models off from foreign nationals, including its own staff, which forced a shutdown for everyone. Ben Thompson says Anthropic’s safety story and its business are the same thing: sell “we are the adults” to companies, and every Washington fight is also marketing. Adversaries will build the weapons anyway. This is political heat, not factory heat.
Force: a private lab that claims a veto on national power will get treated as a national-security object.
From: Stratechery Anthropic and alignment, Anthropic’s safety superpower, Who’s afraid of Chinese models.
13
Rates up, the job already dead
US trucking rates jumped because they kicked supply out (licenses, language tests, lawsuit risk), not because the economy came back. Drivers are more miserable than when rates were low: cameras and speed limiters killed the cowboy job. Parking is short. Brokers need a safe-harbor checkbox after huge verdicts. One industry so far. The pattern to watch: the price of the work goes up while the life of the worker gets worse, because the state is shrinking who is allowed to do it.
Force: supply was chopped by rule, not by demand. Pain sits under this. The story is the pattern.
From: Odd Lots 13 Aug. Pain stream stays separate.
14
Models start doing AI research
Ryan Greenblatt’s median: once models match top human AI researchers, better models make better models, and four or five years of progress pack into one year. Dwarkesh is skeptical. One guest is not a peak. If it starts, every other card on this board moves faster. Until there is a second serious voice plus a measurement, it stays emerging and uncertain.
Force: the research loop, if it closes, is a different species of trend. It changes the clock.
From: Dwarkesh 11 Aug, Ryan Greenblatt.
